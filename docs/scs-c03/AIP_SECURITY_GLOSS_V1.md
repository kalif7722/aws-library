### Retain identity; let presentation adapt

Keep the navy AIP family, readable white type, purposeful amber/cyan/violet accents, recognizable service imagery, accurate arrows and balanced teaching density. Use brighter service colors and restrained dimensional accents where they help students follow the mechanism.

Let title alignment, number of regions, reading direction, panel usage, icon treatment, visual scale, example placement and footer presence adapt to the topic. A diagram may be open on the navy field, organized inside a meaningful account/service boundary, asymmetrical around a central mechanism, or supported by a small example. Panels group related information; they are not the subject of the lesson.

Do not apply the same shell to every slide:
- No automatic top row of three numbered stages, equal card grid or repeated source-processor-output pipeline.
- No mandatory large rounded container, pill-shaped heading, glass border around every object or bottom takeaway/caution strip.
- No uniform thick glow, oversized service tiles or decorative person/hand illustrations on every slide.
- No default table. Use one when precise comparison or exact values are the teaching task; combine it with a visual only when both earn their space.
- No fixed rotation of layouts merely for variety. Repeat a structure when the content genuinely benefits from it, and explain that choice.

### Decide before prompting

For each lesson, write the teaching claim and the relationship students must see: cause/effect, sequence, scope, trust boundary, transformation, alternatives, request/response, failure evidence or recovery. Select a composition that makes that relationship visible. Identify the main visual focal point, exact labels, one useful example and the intended reading order. Choose the two structurally closest shared references.

Describe the mechanism first in the image prompt. Describe AIP styling afterward as a shared visual language. Explicitly state any unnecessary devices to omit for that lesson, such as tables, footer strips or enclosing cards. Avoid a universal long prompt that forces the same decorative elements onto every subject.

### Batch review gate

Review candidates beside the original shared references and the preceding accepted slides. Ask whether the student can explain the concept by tracing the picture, whether the chosen layout fits the teaching relationship, and whether decorative repetition is making different topics look the same. Preserve useful explanation; remove repeated framing before shrinking text. Reject semantically unrelated motifs and decorative variation without educational value.

Record composition, reason for choosing it, reference pair, essential facts and QA result in the production sheet. A screenshot-style walkthrough should be organized around the actual console task; a policy decision around its gates; an architecture around real boundaries and paths.
